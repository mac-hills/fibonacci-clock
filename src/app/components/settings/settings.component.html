Understanding Time with the Fibonacci Clock

The Fibonacci Clock is based on the mathematical sequence created by the Italian mathematician Fibonacci in the 13th century. Beginning with 1 and 1, each subsequent number is the sum of the two preceding ones. The clock uses the initial terms of this sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, and 34.

Shapes

The clock display consists of shapes, each sized according to the Fibonacci numbers: The smallest shape represents 1. The largest shape represents 34. The other shapes represent 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, and 21, arranged from smallest to largest. These shapes are displayed on the clock face and are color-coded to represent hours, minutes, and seconds.

Calculate Time

Hour: Add up the values of the shapes depicted in the colors that represent the hour including the values of the shapes that represent hours and minutes.

Minutes: Add up the values of the shapes depicted in the colors that represent the minutes including the shapes that represent hours and minutes.

Seconds: Seconds are represented by the small circle that changes color every second. Additionally, the background transitions from one color to another after a minute passes, it then it returns to its original color.

Practice Reading the Clock

If you're new to the Fibonacci Clock, you can enable the calculation panel in the display settings tab in the menu at the top. This panel shows the calculations for the current time, making it easier to practice and learn how to read the clock.
